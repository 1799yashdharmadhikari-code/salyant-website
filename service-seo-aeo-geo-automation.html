Key Takeaway for Decision Makers

What should an AI SEO programme actually improve?

It should improve the things a business can inspect and control: technical accessibility, page clarity, internal linking, helpful answers, structured information, source quality and the way progress is measured. AEO and GEO are useful lenses for question-led and AI-assisted discovery, but they do not create a separate shortcut around search fundamentals.

Salyant starts with observable website evidence, reviews the commercial impact, prioritises the work and agrees an implementation scope. Signal provides the diagnostic layer; any client-side system connection or production publishing is separately assessed and supervised.

Why combine SEO, AEO and GEO in one operating plan?

These disciplines overlap. Technical accessibility, clear page structure, useful content, reliable source information and strong internal linking help visitors and search systems understand the same website. Managing them together makes it easier to prioritise improvements around a shared business objective rather than treating each acronym as a separate product.

The work starts by mapping the current pages and the questions they serve. External competitor and search-result observations can inform research, but they are snapshots rather than proof of a stable Google position. Recommendations are checked for evidence, relevance and overlap before they become implementation tasks.

  • One joined-up plan for technical SEO, answer clarity and generative-search context.
  • Shared priorities across content, internal links, structured information and site health.
  • Evidence-led recommendations mapped to the pages and audiences they affect.
  • Measurement that separates diagnostics, search performance and qualified business outcomes.

How an AI SEO and search-visibility programme works

A useful programme starts with a defined website, audience, service priorities and target questions. Research then reviews the available technical evidence, the current page set and the search language used by potential customers. External search results are treated as observations, not proof of stable Google positions or market share.

The team maps findings to existing pages before proposing new content. Each recommendation needs a reason to exist: it should answer a distinct buyer question, improve an identifiable technical issue or strengthen a relevant service page. Similar pages are consolidated or differentiated to avoid keyword cannibalisation.

  • Technical SEO checks for crawlability, indexing signals, page structure, canonical and sitemap hygiene.
  • AI-assisted keyword and competitor research, with source URLs retained for review.
  • AEO improvements that answer genuine customer questions clearly and accurately.
  • GEO work that strengthens entity clarity, source evidence and consistent machine-readable context.
  • Human review, approval and post-change verification for any production implementation.

Salyant uses automation to support research and repeatable analysis. The specific schedule, integrations, deliverables and publishing permissions for a client are agreed during onboarding; this service page does not imply that every client receives an identical daily auto-publishing system.

Governance: agree what may change before anything goes live

Automation should reduce repetitive work without removing accountability. Before implementation, Salyant and the website owner agree the target environment, permitted actions, access level, review responsibility and rollback approach. Research or a generated draft is not the same as a change deployed to a live website.

  • Least-privilege access and a named owner for each connected property.
  • Human review of commercial claims, facts, brand language and material page changes.
  • Explicit approval before production publishing unless a different control is agreed and tested in writing.
  • Validation after deployment, with an agreed correction or rollback route.
  • No fabricated testimonials, performance results, rankings or guarantees.

Client implementation capability is confirmed as part of a scoped pilot, not assumed from a marketing description.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is AI SEO?

AI SEO uses AI-assisted research and analysis to support established search optimisation: technical SEO, useful content, internal linking, structured information and measurement. AI should improve the workflow, not replace editorial judgement or search fundamentals.

How do AEO and GEO relate to AI SEO?

AEO focuses on clear, useful answers to real questions. GEO focuses on helping generative search systems understand the business, its services and its evidence. Both complement technical SEO and helpful content.

Does Salyant provide fully autonomous AI SEO publishing?

Salyant provides AI-assisted research, diagnosis, prioritisation and scoped implementation. Customer CMS integrations and unattended autonomous publishing are not self-serve Signal features. Any production changes require agreed access, scope, approval and verification.

What does the AI Search Growth Blueprint include?

It includes human-reviewed findings, commercial-impact assessment, a prioritised roadmap and implementation recommendations. Its fee is credited against the first agreed implementation engagement commissioned from the roadmap.

How much do AI SEO services cost?

The indicative Blueprint is £500–£1,500 ex VAT. Managed optimisation is £1,500–£3,500+ ex VAT per month. Implementation and portfolio programmes are separately scoped.

Can an AI SEO agency guarantee Google rankings or ChatGPT citations?

No. Search engines and AI systems control crawling, ranking, selection and citations. Salyant measures agreed technical and content improvements, reports observable evidence and avoids promises outside its control.
