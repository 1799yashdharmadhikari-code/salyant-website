Answer Engine Optimisation (AEO): how to make your business easier to understand and cite

People increasingly ask search and AI assistants full questions rather than typing short keyword strings. AEO is the practice of making the answers on your website clear, accurate and easy to verify—without pretending that any website can control what an answer engine selects.

Key takeaway

Answer Engine Optimisation (AEO) is not a schema trick or a promise of ChatGPT citations. It is a disciplined approach to understanding customer questions, writing direct and complete answers, making supporting evidence accessible and keeping business information consistent. Strong technical SEO remains part of the work.

What is Answer Engine Optimisation?

Answer Engine Optimisation, commonly shortened to AEO, describes work that helps a website answer the questions customers ask. Those questions might concern a service, a process, a price range, eligibility, risks or how one option differs from another. A useful page makes the answer easy to find and gives enough context for the reader to assess it.

The phrase is used across the search industry, and providers do not all define it in the same way. In practical terms, it overlaps with helpful content, technical SEO, information architecture and clear source attribution. It should not be treated as a separate ranking system with a guaranteed result.

How AEO differs from SEO and GEO

SEO: technical and organic search foundations

Search engine optimisation covers crawlability, indexing, page relevance, internal linking, site quality and the usefulness of content. It helps people and search systems find and understand the right page.

AEO: direct answers to real questions

AEO focuses on whether the site clearly answers customer questions. That includes definitions, process explanations, practical comparisons, limitations and next steps.

GEO: context for generative search

Generative Engine Optimisation (GEO) focuses on how the business, its services and its supporting evidence are understood in generative search experiences. It often depends on the same accessible content and trustworthy context as SEO and AEO.

Build pages around questions customers actually ask

Start with conversations from sales calls, support messages, proposals, search queries and customer interviews. Group them by intent. A person asking “what is AEO?” needs an explanation; someone asking “AEO agency UK” is evaluating providers; a buyer asking how AEO affects a regulated business may need a more specific service page.

Do not make a separate page for every phrase variation. Decide which page should own each important question. Use supporting articles to explain adjacent topics and link them to the relevant service page. This reduces duplication and gives visitors a logical path from learning to evaluation.

Write answers that are clear, useful and attributable

  • Answer the question early, then explain the reasoning and important qualifications.
  • Use specific headings that describe the content below them.
  • Distinguish facts, opinions, estimates and examples.
  • Identify sources for technical, legal or statistical statements.
  • Use first-party evidence where available, but do not imply client results that cannot be substantiated.
  • Keep names, services, locations and organisation details consistent across the site.

A short answer block can help a reader orient themselves, but it should not replace the complete explanation. The best content covers the important caveats, decision criteria and practical implications rather than repeating a question with a one-line response.

Make content accessible to search crawlers

Important information should be available as readable page content, linked from relevant pages and not hidden exclusively inside an image or an inaccessible interaction. Review robots directives, canonical URLs, redirects, sitemap entries, page status codes and internal links. A blocked crawler cannot retrieve content simply because a page includes an AI-friendly description.

Structured data can describe visible entities and page types when it accurately reflects the page. It is not a guarantee of a featured result or citation. Do not add markup for claims, ratings, services or FAQs that users cannot actually see on the page.

What about ChatGPT SEO and AI citations?

“ChatGPT SEO” is a phrase people use when they want their business to appear in answers generated by ChatGPT and similar systems. There is no reliable setting that forces an assistant to mention a company. A site owner can improve the quality, clarity and accessibility of its source information, but cannot control the model's retrieval, ranking or citation decision.

For this reason, any AI-visibility monitoring should be described as an observation. Record the query or prompt, date, product, locale and source URL. Repeat checks consistently and distinguish an observed citation from a broad claim that a brand is now visible across AI search.

Common AEO mistakes

Publishing thin question pages

Creating many near-identical FAQs can make a site harder to navigate and does not create genuine expertise. Consolidate overlapping questions and answer them in the right context.

Using schema as a shortcut

Structured data should match visible content and valid entities. It cannot compensate for weak page information or make an unsupported claim trustworthy.

Making unprovable visibility claims

Do not promise a fixed number of ChatGPT citations, AI Overview placements or search ranking gains. These are outcomes controlled by external systems and changing conditions.

Ignoring the commercial journey

Answer a buyer's question and provide a relevant next step. A helpful guide should connect to the service that can solve the problem, not force every reader into a generic contact form.

A practical AEO checklist for a UK business

  • List the main services, audiences, locations and questions the business can credibly answer.
  • Map each question to an existing page before creating a new one.
  • Check technical access, page titles, headings, canonicals and internal links.
  • Write direct answers supported by clear explanations and source evidence.
  • Validate structured data against what a visitor can see.
  • Measure search performance and qualified enquiries using connected data sources.
  • Review AI-answer observations with dates and source URLs; avoid treating them as guaranteed results.

Frequently asked questions

What is AEO in digital marketing?

AEO is Answer Engine Optimisation: making the information on a website clear and useful for questions people ask search engines and answer systems.

Is AEO the same as SEO?

No. AEO focuses more directly on question-answer usefulness, while SEO covers broader technical and organic search foundations. The two overlap and should work together.

How do I appear in ChatGPT answers?

You cannot guarantee a mention. You can make your business information accurate, accessible, specific and supported by credible evidence, then monitor citations as an observation.

Does FAQ schema guarantee AI citations?

No. Structured data can describe visible content when used correctly, but it does not guarantee search features or AI citations.

Should every question have its own page?

No. Group related questions and answer them on the most relevant service or guide page. Create a separate page only when the intent and value are genuinely distinct.
